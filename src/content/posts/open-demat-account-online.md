A **demat account** holds your shares and other investments in electronic form, and a **trading account** lets you buy and sell them. Today most brokers open both together, fully online, in about 15 minutes, using your PAN and Aadhaar. Here's the whole process.

## What you need

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| **PAN card** | Required for every investment account. Must be linked with Aadhaar. |
| **Aadhaar** | Your mobile number must be linked to it, to receive the e-sign OTP. |
| **Bank account in your name** | For adding money and receiving withdrawals. |
| **Signature** | Some brokers ask for a photo of your signature on white paper. |
| **Income proof** | Only if you want to trade futures & options or commodities. |

You must be **18 or older**. The **name on your PAN, Aadhaar and bank account should match**. Different spellings are the most common reason applications get stuck.

## Step 1: Choose a SEBI-registered broker

Only open an account with a broker registered with **SEBI** (the Securities and Exchange Board of India). Groww and Upstox are both SEBI-registered and popular with first-time investors:

- **Groww**: simple app, popular for mutual funds and long-term investing.
- **Upstox**: popular with traders for its charts and tools.

Both have offered free online account opening. Check each broker's current charges (brokerage, demat maintenance fees) before you choose.

## Step 2: Sign up through the app

Install the broker's official app. If you use a referral link, open it **before** you install or sign up so the referral is recorded. Then verify your mobile number and email with OTP.

## Step 3: Enter your PAN and personal details

You'll enter your PAN, date of birth, and details like occupation, income range and trading experience. These are regulatory questions, so answer honestly. They don't affect approval for normal investing.

## Step 4: Complete KYC with Aadhaar

Most brokers use **DigiLocker or Aadhaar OTP** to fetch your address and verify your identity:

1. Approve the DigiLocker or Aadhaar request.
2. Take a short **selfie or "in-person verification" video** in the app.
3. Add your **bank account**: usually verified by a ₹1 deposit or by entering the account number and IFSC.

## Step 5: E-sign the application

Your application form is signed digitally with an **Aadhaar OTP** sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile. No paper or courier is needed.

## Step 6: Wait for activation

The broker verifies your documents and opens your demat account with a depository (CDSL or NSDL). This usually takes **one or two working days**. You'll get an email with your client ID and demat details (your BO ID).

## Common mistakes that cause delays

- **PAN not linked with Aadhaar.** An unlinked PAN becomes inoperative, so link it first on the Income Tax e-filing portal.
- **Mobile not linked to Aadhaar.** Without it you can't e-sign with OTP, and the slower offline route is needed.
- **Name mismatch.** Example: "Rahul Kumar" on PAN but "Rahul K" at the bank.
- **Blurry photos.** Retake signature or document photos in good light.
- **Someone else's bank account.** The bank account must be in your own name.

## After your account opens

- **Start small.** Learn how orders, charges and taxes work before investing large amounts.
- **Prefer long-term investing to trading.** SEBI studies have found that most individual traders in futures and options lose money.
- **Turn on two-factor login and never share OTPs.** Ignore "tips" groups that promise guaranteed returns.

Opening through a referral link can also get you the broker's current new-user reward. Treat that as a small bonus, not a reason to invest. The steps to claim it are on the app pages below.
